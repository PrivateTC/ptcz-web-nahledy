Český Krumlov sits about two and a half hours south of Prague. Most people come for the castle — the second largest in the country — but the quieter pleasure is the old town itself: an almost completely preserved medieval and Renaissance townscape, wrapped so tightly by the Vltava that it feels like an island. We time the day so you arrive before the coaches and have the morning light to yourself, then loop back through the South Bohemian countryside, with the option of a second castle on the way home.

StopsWhat you'll see

Stop 1 · about 3 h

Český Krumlov old town & castle UNESCO

The castle complex (40 buildings on a rocky promontory), the painted Renaissance tower, the tiered Baroque gardens, and the lanes of the old town ending at the river on three sides. Time for the castle interior, a riverside lunch, or simply wandering.

Optional stop · about 1 h 30

Hluboká Castle Add-on on the way back

A neo-Gothic castle modelled on Windsor — bright white above the Vltava valley, with a well-preserved interior and an English-style park. Easy to add on the drive home if you'd like a second castle; skip it for a slower day in Krumlov.

How to visit Český Krumlov from Prague (private vs group vs self-driving)

It's about 170 km (2 h 30) each way. A group coach is cheapest but rigid — fixed departure, ~4 hours on site, no stops. Self-driving works if you're comfortable parking outside the centre and want full freedom. A private car sits in between on price and at the top on comfort: door-to-door from your hotel, an English-speaking driver, your own timing, and the option to add Hluboká or a viewpoint without renegotiating. For a couple or a family who value a relaxed day, private usually wins; for a solo traveller on a tight budget, the bus is fine.

Cost & what's included

From €429 per car (Škoda Superb, 1–3 guests; Hyundai Staria up to 8 from €572) — one price for the whole car, not per person. It includes the private vehicle, driver, fuel, motorway tolls and parking. It does not include castle interior tickets, meals or drinks. No deposit — you pay the driver.

Best time to visit

May, June and September are the sweet spot — long days, open interiors, manageable crowds. July–August is busiest; we counter it by starting early. Winter is quiet and atmospheric (snow on the rooftops, Christmas markets) but some interiors close. The early-morning trick matters all year: arriving by 9–9:30 gives you the old town before the coaches.

With kids / as a couple

For couples we lean into the slow version: a long lunch, the gardens, a viewpoint at golden hour. For families, the castle tower, the bear moat and ice-cream by the river keep younger travellers happy; child seats are provided on request. Either way the pace bends to you.

Tickets & opening days (we check before you go)

Castle interior tours run roughly April–October and require timed tickets in peak season — we arrange them. Interiors are typically closed on Mondays outside summer, and in winter many close entirely; the town and exteriors are lovely year-round. If a closure would spoil your plan, we'll tell you and suggest a better day — we'd rather you have the right trip than just sell one.

Frequently asked questions

How long do we need? A full day does it justice; a half-day feels rushed once you add travel.

Is the driver also a guide? Your driver is a knowledgeable local; a licensed guide for the castle interior can be arranged.

Can we change the plan on the day? Yes — add a stop, linger longer, head back early. No surcharge.

What language? English throughout; some of our drivers also speak German.
